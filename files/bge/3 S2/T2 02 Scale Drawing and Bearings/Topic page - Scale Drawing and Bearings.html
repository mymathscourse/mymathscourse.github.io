            Week 4

            Plan the classroom

            A classroom is 9 m long and 7 m wide. You want to draw a plan on A4 paper (29.7 cm by 21 cm), leaving a small border.

            Which scale makes the plan as big as possible? Try 1 : 20, 1 : 30, 1 : 40 and 1 : 50.

            Answer1 : 30 gives 30 cm by 23.3 cm, which is too big. 1 : 40 gives 22.5 cm by 17.5 cm, which fits with a border, so it is the biggest. 1 : 50 (18 cm by 14 cm) also fits and is easier to work out: 1 cm stands for 0.5 m.
            Week 4

            Twice as big?

            Draw a 3 cm by 2 cm rectangle. Enlarge it by scale factor 2, then by scale factor 3.

            What happens to the perimeter each time? What happens to the area? Can you predict scale factor 4?

            AnswerPerimeter: 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm, so it is multiplied by the scale factor. Area: 6 cm², 24 cm², 54 cm², so it is multiplied by the scale factor squared (× 4, × 9). Scale factor 4: perimeter 40 cm, area 6 × 16 = 96 cm².
            Week 5

            Why ± 180°?

            The bearing of B from A is 070°. Draw it, with a North line at A and a North line at B.

            Use the diagram to explain why the bearing of A from B is 250°. Why do North lines have to be parallel?

            AnswerCarry the line AB on past B: it still points on 070°. Turning round to face A is a half turn, 180°, so 070° + 180° = 250°. North lines all point the same way (to the North Pole, which is very far away), so they are parallel. That is why the angles match at A and B.
            Week 5

            Bearing riddles

            1. I face 300°. I turn 90° clockwise, then 90° clockwise again. Which bearing do I face now?

            2. Can the bearing of B from A ever be the same as the bearing of A from B?

            3. Make up a riddle of your own for a partner.

            Answer1. 300 + 180 = 480, and 480 − 360 = 120°. 2. No: they always differ by exactly 180°.
            Week 6

            Rescue at sea

            Coastguard station B is 10 km due East of station A. A boat in trouble is on a bearing of 060° from A and 300° from B.

            Make a scale drawing (1 cm : 1 km) to find where the boat is. How far is it from each station?

            AnswerThe lines cross about 5.8 km from each station. The triangle is isosceles: the angles at A and B are both 30° (90 − 60 and 300 − 270), so the boat is the same distance from both.
            Week 6

            Impossible triangles

            Try to construct triangles with these sides: (a) 3, 4, 5 cm; (b) 2, 3, 6 cm; (c) 5, 5, 10 cm; (d) 4, 4, 4 cm.

            Which ones are impossible? Write a rule that tells you before you start.

            Answer(b) and (c) are impossible. In (b) the arcs never meet; in (c) the "triangle" is squashed flat into a straight line. Rule: the two shorter sides must add up to more than the longest side.
            Challenge

            Orienteering loop

            A loop has three legs of 2 km each and finishes back at the start. The first leg is on a bearing of 060°.

            What are the bearings of the other two legs? Check with a scale drawing.

            Answer180° and 300°. The route is an equilateral triangle, so you turn 120° clockwise at each corner: 060 + 120 = 180, 180 + 120 = 300.

            Spot the mistake

            Who is right? What went wrong?

            Read each one on your own first, then explain the mistake to a partner. How would you help the pupil fix it?

            Mistake 1

            Sam's bearing

            Sam says the bearing of B from A is 050°.

            AnswerSam measured anticlockwise. Bearings are always measured clockwise from North, so it is 360 − 50 = 310°.
            Mistake 2

            Aisha's East

            Aisha writes: "The bearing of East is 90°."

            AnswerThe angle is right, but bearings always have three figures: 090°.
            Mistake 3

            Jack's way back

            The bearing of B from A is 070°. Jack says the bearing of A from B must also be 070°.

            AnswerGoing back is a half turn, so add 180°: the bearing of A from B is 250°.
            Mistake 4

            Ross's map

            A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. Ross says 4 cm on the map is 200 000 km.

            Answer4 × 50 000 = 200 000 is right, but it is 200 000 cm, not km. 200 000 cm = 2000 m = 2 km.
            Mistake 5

            Mia's area

            Mia enlarges a 3 cm by 2 cm rectangle by scale factor 2. She says the area doubles from 6 cm² to 12 cm².

            AnswerBoth lengths double, so the new rectangle is 6 cm by 4 cm and its area is 24 cm². The area is multiplied by 2 × 2 = 4.
            Mistake 6

            Callum's protractor

            Callum read the outside scale of his protractor and wrote 050°.

            AnswerHe read the wrong scale. The angle is obtuse (bigger than 90°), so it can't be 050°. Start from 0 on the North line: it is 130°.
