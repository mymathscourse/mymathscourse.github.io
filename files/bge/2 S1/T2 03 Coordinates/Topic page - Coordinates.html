          Four quadrants

          Where could D go?

          A(4, 1), B(1, −1) and C(−4, 1) are three corners of a parallelogram. Plot them on a grid from −8 to 10.

          Find a fourth corner D. Then find all the other places D could go.

          AnswerThere are three places: (−1, 3), (9, −1) and (−7, −1). For ABCD, D = (−1, 3): from B to A is 3 right and 2 up, so from C go 3 right and 2 up.
          Four quadrants

          Same x, same y

          A(3, −9) · B(−7, −9) · C(3, 4)
          D(3, 12) · E(−2, −9) · F(3, 7)

          Which points have the same x-coordinate? Which have the same y-coordinate? What do you notice about where they are on a grid?

          AnswerSame x-coordinate (3): A, C, D and F. They are all on a vertical line (straight up and down). Same y-coordinate (−9): A, B and E. They are all on a horizontal line (straight across).
          Fractions

          Fraction coordinates

          Draw a grid 6 squares across and 6 squares up. Mark 0 at the origin and 1 after 4 squares on both axes.

          Plot (1, 1), (½, 1), (1¼, ¾) and (1½, 1¼).

          Extra: what is each square worth? Where would (¾, 1½) go?

          AnswerEach square is worth ¼. (1, 1): 4 along, 4 up. (½, 1): 2 along, 4 up. (1¼, ¾): 5 along, 3 up. (1½, 1¼): 6 along, 5 up. (¾, 1½) is 3 along, 6 up.
          Challenge

          Round and round

          1. Rotate the point (2, 5) a quarter turn (90°) clockwise about the origin. Do it again, and again, and again. Write down each point. What do you notice?

          2. The midpoint is halfway between two points. Find the midpoint of (−2, 1) and (6, 5).

          Answer1. (5, −2), (−2, −5), (−5, 2), then back to (2, 5). The four points are the corners of a square. 2. (2, 3): halfway between −2 and 6 is 2, halfway between 1 and 5 is 3.
