          Week 4

          Same perimeter, different area

          Draw every rectangle with whole-number sides that has a perimeter of 24 cm. Work out the area of each one.

          Which rectangle has the biggest area? Which has the smallest? What do you notice?

          AnswerLength + width = 12. 1 × 11 = 11 cm², 2 × 10 = 20 cm², 3 × 9 = 27 cm², 4 × 8 = 32 cm², 5 × 7 = 35 cm², 6 × 6 = 36 cm². The square (6 cm × 6 cm) has the biggest area; the long thin 1 cm × 11 cm rectangle has the smallest.
          Week 4

          Same area, different perimeter

          Find every rectangle with whole-number sides that has an area of 36 cm². Work out the perimeter of each one.

          Which has the shortest perimeter? Why would a farmer care about this when building a pen?

          Answer1 × 36 (74 cm), 2 × 18 (40 cm), 3 × 12 (30 cm), 4 × 9 (26 cm), 6 × 6 (24 cm). The square has the shortest perimeter, so it needs the least fencing for the same space.
          Challenge

          Cutting a square

          A square is cut into two rectangles with one straight cut. The perimeters of the two rectangles are 16 cm and 20 cm.

          What is the area of the square?

          Answer36 cm². The square is 6 cm by 6 cm. It is cut into a 2 cm × 6 cm rectangle (perimeter 16 cm) and a 4 cm × 6 cm rectangle (perimeter 20 cm).
          Challenge

          Biggest and smallest area

          The length of a rectangle is 3.1 cm (to 1 decimal place). The width is 8.4 cm (to 1 decimal place).

          What is the biggest possible area of the rectangle? What is the smallest possible area?

          AnswerThe length is between 3.05 cm and 3.15 cm, and the width is between 8.35 cm and 8.45 cm. Smallest area: 3.05 × 8.35 = 25.4675 cm². Biggest area: just under 3.15 × 8.45 = 26.6175 cm².
