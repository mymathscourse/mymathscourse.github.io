          Talk 1

          Count the squares

          1. How many small squares are in the blue square? The red square?
          2. How many are in the purple square? How could you check, when it is tilted?
          3. What do you notice? Will it work for any right-angled triangle?
          AnswerBlue 9, red 16, purple 25, and 9 + 16 = 25. Count the whole squares in the purple square, then fit the part squares together. It works for every right-angled triangle: that is Pythagoras' theorem, a² + b² = c².
          Talk 2

          Two big squares

          1. The two big squares are the same size. What is the same in both pictures? What is different?
          2. Each grey triangle has shorter sides a and b and hypotenuse c. Where can you see a², b² and c²?
          3. Explain why the purple area must equal the blue and red areas together.
          AnswerBoth big squares have sides a + b and both contain the same four grey triangles. Take the triangles away from each: on the left you are left with the purple square c², on the right with the blue square a² and the red square b². The same amount was taken from the same size of square, so c² = a² + b².
          Talk 3

          Odd one out

          Triangles with these sides (in cm):

          6, 8, 10  ·  5, 12, 13  ·  4, 5, 6  ·  9, 12, 15

          1. Which is the odd one out? Convince me.
          2. Can you find a reason for a different one to be the odd one out?
          Answer4, 5, 6 is the only one that isn't right-angled: 4² + 5² = 41 but 6² = 36. Other answers: 5, 12, 13 is the only right-angled one that isn't a multiple of 3, 4, 5; 6, 8, 10 is the only one where every side is even. Accept any reason that is convincing.
          Talk 4

          Not just squares?

          Instead of squares, draw a semicircle on each side of a 3, 4, 5 triangle.

          1. Do you think the two smaller semicircles add up to the biggest one?
          2. What about equilateral triangles on each side?
          3. Work out the areas to check. What do you notice?
          AnswerYes, both times. The semicircles have areas π × 1.5² ÷ 2 = 3.53, π × 2² ÷ 2 = 6.28 and π × 2.5² ÷ 2 = 9.82 cm², and 3.53 + 6.28 = 9.82 (rounded). Any shape works, as long as the three shapes are similar (the same shape, enlarged), because each area is the side² times the same number.
