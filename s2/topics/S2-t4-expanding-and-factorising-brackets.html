Expanding and Factorising Brackets

What you'll learn

Week of 12 April 2027

Expanding and Factorising Brackets

  • I can use the correct order of operations, including with brackets, e.g. 20 − 3(4 − 6)²
  • I can multiply algebraic terms, e.g. 3a × 4b, 2x × 5x
  • I can expand a single bracket, e.g. 4(2x − 3)
  • I can expand with a negative or algebraic multiplier, e.g. −3(x − 5), x(2x + 7)
  • I can expand and simplify, e.g. 3(x + 4) − 2(x − 1)
Curriculum links (Experiences and Outcomes)
  • MTH 4-14a Having explored the distributive law in practical contexts, I can simplify, multiply and evaluate simple algebraic terms involving a bracket.
  • MTH 4-03b I have investigated how introducing brackets to an expression can change the emphasis and can demonstrate my understanding by using the correct order of operations when carrying out calculations.

Week of 19 April 2027

Expanding and Factorising Brackets

  • I can expand double brackets, e.g. (x + 3)(x − 5)
  • I can square a bracket, e.g. (2x − 1)²
  • I can expand a bracket with three terms, e.g. (x + 2)(x² − 3x + 4)
  • I can evaluate expressions by substitution, including negative numbers

Week of 26 April 2027

Expanding and Factorising Brackets

  • I can find the highest common factor of numbers and algebraic terms
  • I can factorise using a common factor, e.g. 6x + 9, 4ab − 10a
  • I can factorise fully, including a common squared term, e.g. 3x² + 12x
  • I can check a factorisation by expanding
Curriculum links (Experiences and Outcomes)
  • MTH 4-14b I can find the factors of algebraic terms, use my understanding to identify common factors and apply this to factorise expressions.
